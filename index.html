Disease Overview

RSV Pathophysiology & Clinical Spectrum

Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV) is a single-stranded, negative-sense RNA virus (Pneumoviridae family) that infects nearly all children by age 2, and causes recurrent infections throughout life. Its fusion (F) protein is the primary target for prevention strategies.

🧬

Viral Mechanism

RSV F protein mediates fusion with host respiratory epithelial cells. Pre-fusion conformation (pre-F) is the key neutralization target exploited by nirsevimab and vaccine antigens.

💨

Transmission

Droplet and contact spread (fomites). Incubation 2–8 days. Viral shedding persists up to 3–8 days in immunocompetent hosts; weeks in immunocompromised.

🛡️

Immune Evasion

NS1/NS2 proteins suppress IFN-α/β responses. Incomplete immune memory leads to repeated infections across all ages. Maternal antibodies wane by 4–6 months.

🔥

Inflammatory Cascade

Airway inflammation, mucus hypersecretion, and bronchospasm drive bronchiolitis. Th2-skewed response in infants amplifies wheeze and may predispose to later asthma.

Clinical Spectrum of RSV Disease

Mild Moderate Severe
URTI

Rhinorrhea, cough, low-grade fever. Self-limited in healthy adults & older children. 2–7 days.

Bronchiolitis

Infants <12 mo. Wheezing, tachypnea, feeding difficulty. Leading cause of infant hospitalization.

Pneumonia

RSV LRTI in elderly, immunocompromised. Hypoxia, consolidation. High 30-day mortality in 60+.

Severe LRTI

Respiratory failure, NIV/MV requirement. Associated with cardiac decompensation in older adults.

Complications by Population

INFANTS

Apnea, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, reactive airway disease, prolonged hospitalization, PICU admission

ELDERLY / HIGH-RISK

COPD/asthma exacerbations, heart failure decompensation, prolonged functional decline, 30-day mortality 3–7%

Saudi Epidemiology

Regional Burden & Co-morbidity Context

Saudi Arabia's unique demographic and climatic profile — high prevalence of non-communicable diseases, large congregating events (Hajj, Umrah), and a growing elderly population — amplifies RSV burden beyond global averages.

Hajj & Umrah — Mass Gathering Risk

Approximately 1.8–2.5 million pilgrims annually create conditions for amplified respiratory virus transmission. RSV co-circulates with influenza, rhinovirus, and other respiratory pathogens. Elderly pilgrims and those with comorbidities are at substantially elevated risk of severe RSV disease during congregational activities.

Mass Gathering Amplifier

⚠ MERS-CoV & Co-circulation Context

Saudi Arabia accounts for >85% of globally reported MERS-CoV cases. Concurrent MERS and RSV infection in immunocompromised and elderly patients has been documented, with synergistic immune impairment. RSV surveillance programs must account for dual respiratory pathogen risk in KSA healthcare settings.

Aging Population — Growing Burden

Adults aged 60+ represent ~7% of KSA's population today, projected to reach 15% by 2040. Combined with high comorbidity rates, this demographic shift will substantially increase RSV-attributable hospitalization burden, underscoring the urgency of adult RSV vaccination programs.

Pregnant Women

Serve as the immunization bridge. Maternal RSV vaccine generates IgG that crosses the placenta, providing passive protection to infants during peak vulnerability.

  • Vaccination at 32–36 weeks gestation (Abrysvo)

Prevention Strategies

Evidence-Based Prevention Approaches

A tiered prevention framework exists for RSV, tailored by age, risk profile, and available approvals. Select a strategy below to review clinical evidence and dosing guidance.

  • PlatformBivalent recombinant RSV pre-F protein (RSV-A + RSV-B)
  • RouteIM injection, single dose
  • Timing32–36 weeks of gestation

Efficacy vs. Severe RSV LRTI (infants, 180 days) 69.4%
⚠ Safety Note: FDA label includes information about a numerical imbalance in preterm birth observed in MATISSE trial. Timing at 32–36 weeks is critical. Prescribers should evaluate individual benefit-risk.

Mechanism & Clinical Rationale

Maternal immunization generates high-titer RSV-neutralizing antibodies (primarily anti-pre-F IgG) that are actively transported across the placenta via the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). This passive immunization strategy is most effective when vaccination occurs in the third trimester to maximize antibody transfer and cord blood titers.

  • Protects newborns before they can mount their own immune response
  • Antibodies persist for 3–6 months post-birth, covering peak RSV season
  • Particularly valuable in settings where nirsevimab access is limited
  • Complements nirsevimab — not always interchangeable
  • Single dose; no booster required in same pregnancy

MATISSE Trial (Pfizer, n=7,358)

Phase 3 global RCT. Primary endpoint: medically attended severe RSV-associated LRTI in infants through 90 days post-birth. 81.8% VE (95% CI: 40.6–96.3%). Secondary endpoint at 180 days: 69.4% VE against severe disease. Consistent across RSV-A and RSV-B subtypes.

Advantages Over Palivizumab

  • Extended half-life (~70 days) via YTE Fc modification — single-season protection with one dose
  • Targets pre-F conformation — 50–100× greater neutralizing potency than palivizumab
  • Approved for ALL infants in first RSV season (not limited to high-risk)
  • Eligible for all infants born within or entering their first RSV season
  • Can be administered at birth alongside routine vaccines

MELODY Trial (n=1,490) + MEDLEY

MELODY: Term/late preterm infants ≥35 wks. Primary endpoint: medically attended RSV-LRTI. VE 74.5% (95% CI: 49.6–87.1%). MEDLEY extended to preterm 29–34 wks and those with CHD/BPD. Real-world data from France (2023–24 season) demonstrated 83% reduction in hospitalizations.

Clinical Rationale for Adults 60+

  • RSV causes 160,000+ hospitalizations and 14,000 deaths annually in US adults 65+ (comparable to influenza)
  • Immunosenescence reduces natural immune response — vaccine primes pre-F neutralizing responses
  • Arexvy AS01E adjuvant enhances immunogenicity particularly in elderly
  • ACIP recommends shared clinical decision-making for adults 60–74; recommendation for 75+
  • Particular benefit for those with underlying cardiopulmonary conditions, diabetes, CKD

KSA Opportunity: Co-Administration

RSV vaccination can be co-administered with influenza and pneumococcal vaccines during seasonal vaccination campaigns. The existing Hajj/Umrah vaccination infrastructure provides an operational model for reaching adult RSV vaccine target populations.

Supportive Care & Infection Control

No RSV-specific antiviral is approved for general use. Management is supportive, focusing on oxygenation, hydration, and preventing secondary complications.

🫁 Oxygenation

Supplemental O₂ to maintain SpO₂ ≥92–94%. HFNC first-line in infants before CPAP/NIV. Minimize intubation where possible.

💧 Hydration

NG tube feeding preferred over IV fluids in infants with feeding difficulty. Avoid fluid overload. Monitor sodium carefully — SIADH risk.

💊 Bronchodilators

Routine nebulized bronchodilators (salbutamol, epinephrine) NOT recommended for bronchiolitis per AAP/NICE guidelines. Trial-and-continue only if response documented.

⚠️ Corticosteroids

Not recommended for RSV bronchiolitis. May be considered for RSV-triggered severe COPD/asthma exacerbations in adults per standard disease management.

🧪 Ribavirin

Off-label use in severe RSV in HSCT/immunocompromised. Inhaled or oral. Limited evidence. Considered on case-by-case with ID/transplant team.

🧤 Infection Control

Contact + droplet precautions. Cohort RSV patients. Hand hygiene compliance. Visitor restrictions during peak season in NICU/PICU. HCW screening protocols.

Hospital Admission Criteria (Pediatric RSV)

ADMIT

SpO₂ <90%, moderate-severe respiratory distress, age <3 months, prematurity, dehydration, unable to feed, parental concerns

OBSERVE

SpO₂ 90–94%, mild-moderate distress, age 3–6 months, borderline feeding, borderline social situation

DISCHARGE

SpO₂ ≥94% on room air, tolerating feeds, mild symptoms, age >3 months, reliable caregiver with return precautions

ACIP / CDC

United States — ACIP Recommendations

  • ›Nirsevimab: Recommended for all infants <8 months entering first RSV season; 8–19 months if high-risk
  • ›Maternal RSV vaccine (Abrysvo): Recommended 32–36 wks gestation, seasonally (Sep–Jan); or year-round if entering season
  • ›Adults 75+: Single dose RSV vaccine (any approved product) recommended
  • ›Adults 60–74: Shared clinical decision-making based on risk factors
  • ›Maternal vaccine and nirsevimab: one or the other preferred; if maternal vaccine, infant nirsevimab generally not needed
WHO

  • ›WHO SAGE position paper on RSV prevention published 2024 — prioritizes high-burden LMICs
  • ›Maternal RSV immunization recommended where approved and affordable
  • ›Nirsevimab recognized as transformative for infant protection
  • ›Adult RSV vaccine endorsed for 60+ with underlying conditions
  • ›GAVI pathway under discussion for nirsevimab in LMICs — not applicable to KSA but influences regional policy dialogue

European Approvals & Guidance

  • ›Abrysvo approved by EMA for maternal use and adults 60+
  • ›Arexvy (GSK) approved by EMA for adults 60+
  • ›Multiple EU countries (France, Spain, Luxembourg) launched national infant nirsevimab programs 2023–24 with documented real-world effectiveness
  • ›European RSV surveillance programs provide transferable models for KSA

★ Key Messages for KSA Medical Affairs & HCPs

INFANT PROTECTION

Nirsevimab offers the first single-dose, season-long passive immunization for all infants — a paradigm shift beyond palivizumab's high-risk-only scope. Advocate for SFDA approval and NIP inclusion.

MATERNAL PATHWAY

Maternal Abrysvo immunization at 32–36 weeks is actionable now through OB/GYN networks and antenatal clinics. Patient and HCP education is the immediate priority ahead of national guidance.

ADULT BURDEN

RSV in adults 60+ is systematically underdiagnosed. Improving RSV testing in hospitalized elderly patients with respiratory illness is critical for establishing KSA-specific burden data.

SURVEILLANCE

Strengthening sentinel surveillance for RSV seasonality patterns across KSA regions (particularly Makkah, Riyadh, Eastern Province) will refine timing and targeting of prevention interventions.
